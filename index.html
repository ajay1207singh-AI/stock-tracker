        How this learns

        This is a transparent, rules-based scorecard. It is not a prediction model. It does not forecast prices or train on data. It writes down each call, checks it against the market using fixed rules, and keeps a record of which kinds of call worked.

        • Scoring rule. Buy is right if it beats its benchmark by ≥5 percentage points and wrong if it lags by ≥5; anything in between is “in line”. Avoid is the mirror image: right if the stock lags by ≥5 pts. Watchlist is never right or wrong. It is marked “waiting paid” if the price fell, “ran away” if it rose ≥5%, or “triggered” if the entry level was hit.
        • Benchmarks. Nifty 50 for companies with a market cap of ₹1 lakh Cr or more, otherwise the Nifty Smallcap 250. Both are measured from the same reference date as the call.
        • Money view. Each call is worth ₹10,000 notional. Buy counts the gain or loss. Avoid counts the loss avoided or gain missed. Watchlist counts the money saved by waiting or the move missed.
        • Maturity. A call under 30 days old shows “Too early” and is left out of the hit rate (a provisional figure is shown separately). After that, returns are frozen at fixed horizons: +1D, +1W, +1M, +3M, +6M, +1Y, +2Y and +3Y. Each horizon uses the first close on or after the call date plus that many days, taken from the daily price history (history.csv). A horizon shows n/a until it is reached.
        • Sample size today. 106 calls (Buy/Apply 33, Watchlist 6, Avoid 67). 0 have matured past 30 days. Horizon data available so far: +1D 91, +1W 8, +1M 0, +3M 0, +6M 0, +1Y 0, +2Y 0, +3Y 0.
        • Lessons and confidence. A lesson is a pattern across a group of at least 2 calls, such as “Avoided penny stocks: 5/6 fell or lagged”. Each lesson has a confidence level based on how many calls in its group have matured: Very low (none), Low (1–4), Medium (5–14) or High (15+). Every update rewrites lessons.md. When new data flips a lesson (for example, a hit ratio crosses 50%), the old wording is kept in a revision log with the date, so mistakes stay visible.
        Not investment advice. Past hit rates, especially on small and immature samples, say little about future calls.
